Coconut water.
Nature's own
sports drink.

Used across the Caribbean for centuries. Compared against commercial sports drinks in two small clinical trials, with no clear difference found, and easier on the stomach in one of them. A genuinely good source of potassium. Grown here. Available here. Understood here long before sports science had a name for it.

What Coconut Water Is The Science

What Coconut Water Is

Inside the young coconut —a precise biological formulation.

Coconut water is the clear liquid found inside young, green coconuts — harvested before the flesh hardens. It is not coconut milk, which is pressed from mature coconut flesh and is very different in composition. Coconut water is about 95% water, and it carries potassium, sodium, magnesium, calcium and phosphorus. You will often read that its mineral profile mirrors human blood plasma. It does not. Plasma is sodium led, at roughly 135 to 145 millimoles of sodium per litre against about 4 to 5 of potassium. Coconut water runs the other way, potassium led, with many times more potassium than plasma and a good deal less sodium. That makes it a genuinely useful drink. It does not make it a copy of your blood.

The Science

Tested in small trials.Used once or twice in an emergency.

A small trial published in 2012 in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition compared coconut water against a commercial sports drink and against plain water in twelve trained men. It found no measurable difference between them in rehydration or in exercise performance afterwards. That is a fair result, but it is a small study, it was funded by a coconut water company, and some participants reported stomach upset on the coconut drinks. A cup of coconut water does carry about 600mg of potassium against roughly 30mg in a standard cup of a mainstream sports drink. Potassium is not the electrolyte doing most of the work after heavy sweating, though. That job belongs to sodium, which is why sports drinks are built around it, and which is the one thing fresh coconut water is usually short of. Coconut water has been given intravenously in a small number of documented cases where standard medical fluids were not available, including one reported from a hospital in the Solomon Islands in 2000. The widely repeated story that it was used as a battlefield drip during the Second World War is not documented anywhere. The doctors who published that Solomon Islands case called the wartime accounts alleged, and noted they were never formally reported in the medical literature. Doctors also caution that coconut water is too high in potassium to be given into a vein safely in any quantity. It is absorbed well and it goes down easily, which is most of what you want from a recovery drink. Jamaica grows it. Drink it.
