Where to Begin

Start with
mindful eating.

Mindful eating is not a trend or a dietary philosophy. It is a return to how eating was always meant to be — present, deliberate, and connected to the body’s signals. It begins in the mouth.

Chew your food carefully and break it into manageable pieces. This practice helps digestion by allowing saliva and stomach enzymes to work effectively. It also supports oxygen absorption in the small intestine — a key factor in maintaining energy and vitality throughout the day.

Think of your stomach as the foundation — like the roots supporting the branches. It is essential to long-term well-being. When the foundation is cared for, everything built on top of it stands stronger.

01

Slow down before you eat

Take a breath. Acknowledge your food. Rushing into a meal bypasses the body’s preparation signals and reduces digestive efficiency before a single bite is swallowed.

02

Chew thoroughly

Aim for 20–30 chews per mouthful for solid food. Saliva contains amylase — a digestive enzyme that begins breaking down carbohydrates in the mouth, reducing the burden on the stomach.

03

Notice flavour and texture

Taste buds are not passive. They communicate with the brain, signalling what the body is receiving and preparing the digestive system to process it. Paying attention activates this system more fully.

04

Stop when satisfied, not full

The satiety signal takes approximately 20 minutes to reach the brain from the stomach. Eating slowly enough to receive that signal prevents chronic overeating — one of the most common contributors to weight gain.
