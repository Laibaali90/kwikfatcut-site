The Starting Point

Why chewing
thoroughly matters.

Chewing food thoroughly and breaking it into smaller, manageable pieces is essential for proper digestion. It allows saliva — containing enzymes such as amylase — and stomach enzymes such as pepsin to break down nutrients more effectively, directly aiding absorption.

Digestion is not a passive process. It is a cascade. Each stage depends on the one before it. When the first stage is done well, everything that follows is easier. When it is rushed, the entire system carries the burden.

👄

Mouth — Where it begins

Teeth break food mechanically. Saliva introduces amylase, beginning carbohydrate digestion. This stage sets the entire cascade in motion. Rushing it shortens every stage that follows.

🫃

Stomach — Acid & enzyme breakdown

Pepsin and hydrochloric acid break down proteins. The stomach works significantly harder when large, poorly chewed pieces arrive — reducing efficiency and increasing transit time.

🌀

Small intestine — Nutrient absorption

Most nutrients enter the bloodstream here. Inadequately broken-down food limits enzyme access. Poor chewing upstream becomes poor nutrition downstream. Good digestion also supports oxygen absorption in the small intestine — key to maintaining energy and vitality.

♻️

Large intestine — Elimination

Water is reabsorbed. Fibre feeds beneficial gut bacteria. What arrives here reflects everything that happened upstream. A healthy large intestine depends on a healthy process from the very first bite.

When the System Breaks Down

What undigested food
does to the body.

Undigested food can cause serious disruption by blocking enzymes from functioning properly. When large food particles reach the intestines intact, they cannot be broken down efficiently — and consequences build over time.

“Good digestion helps your body absorb nutrients more efficiently. Poor digestion means eating more food — and getting less nutrition.”

The effects are cumulative. Nutrient deficiencies develop silently. The body is fed in calories but starved in vitamins, minerals, and the compounds it needs to repair and protect itself. This is why education about digestion is foundational — not optional.

Signals the Body Sends

Symptoms
to recognise.

Symptoms like bloating, irregular bowel movements, abdominal pain, or discomfort often indicate underlying digestive problems. These are not minor inconveniences. They are the body communicating clearly. It all starts with your stomach hurting.

🫧
Bloating
Often caused by fermentation of undigested food in the gut. Can indicate food intolerances, poor chewing, or enzyme insufficiency.
🔄
Irregular bowel movements
Constipation or diarrhoea are both signals of disrupted digestion — often related to fibre intake, hydration, and food quality.
⚡
Abdominal pain or discomfort
Can signal inflammation, infection, intolerances, or poor transit. Persistent pain always warrants professional assessment.
😮‍💨
Indigestion
Often a direct result of rushed eating, poor chewing, or foods that disrupt stomach acid balance. Frequently preventable through habit changes.
🪫
Persistent fatigue
Poor nutrient absorption leaves cells under-resourced. If digestion is compromised, energy production suffers regardless of how much is eaten.
🧬
Nutrient deficiencies
Iron, B12, vitamin D, and magnesium deficiencies are commonly linked to digestive dysfunction — not just dietary absence.

Food Safety

Certain foods carry
real risks if handled
incorrectly.

Certain foods, whether chewed properly or not, can pose serious health risks when contaminated with harmful bacteria, viruses, or parasites — or when prepared improperly. Understanding which foods require extra care is basic, essential knowledge.

Food Risk / Pathogen Prevention
Raw or undercooked poultry
Salmonella, Campylobacter
Cook to full internal temperature. No pink flesh.
Beef, mutton, pork (rare/medium)
E. coli, Trichinella
Cook thoroughly. Pork especially requires full cooking.
Eggs (undercooked or poorly stored)
Salmonella
Cook yolks fully. Refrigerate properly. Check freshness.
Raw shellfish & seafood
Norovirus, Vibrio, Hepatitis A
Source from reputable suppliers. Cook unless certified safe raw.
Unwashed fruits & vegetables
Giardia, E. coli, pesticide residue
Wash thoroughly under running water before eating or preparing.

Hidden Dangers

Seeds & pits
some fruits conceal.

Some fruits contain seeds or pits that release toxic substances when metabolised. This is not widely known — but it is important, particularly for families with young children.

Be aware

Cherries, apples, apricots, and peaches contain seeds or pits that harbour amygdalin — a compound that, when metabolised, can produce cyanide in the body. In small amounts, accidental ingestion of a single seed is unlikely to cause serious harm. But crushed, cracked, or regularly consumed seeds from these fruits can accumulate to harmful levels — causing constipation, diarrhoea, nausea, and in significant quantities, more serious toxicity.

The fruit flesh itself is safe and nutritious. Simply avoid consuming or crushing the seeds and pits.
