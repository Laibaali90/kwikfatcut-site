Two Vital Fluids

One unified medium.
Expressed two ways.

Water and blood are two vital bodily fluids essential for sustaining and regulating all living organisms. Water, which appears transparent and tasteless, is crucial for numerous biological functions — maintaining cell hydration, facilitating temperature regulation through sweating, and transporting nutrients via the circulatory system. It accounts for approximately 60% of the human body’s weight and participates in countless metabolic reactions within cells.

Blood, a complex and dynamic fluid composed of plasma, red and white blood cells, and platelets, circulates continuously through an intricate network of arteries, veins, and capillaries — enabling oxygen delivery, immune defence, and waste removal. It embodies the perpetual motion of life — never still. This analogy, rooted in ancient traditions like Ayurveda and Egyptian medicine and supported by modern science, underscores the deep interconnectedness and mutual dependency of these two vital fluids.

The Foundation

Water sustains life
through every cell.

Water sustains life by supporting crucial biological processes. The human body, composed of about 60% water by weight, relies on water for cellular hydration, enzymatic reactions such as digestion, and the transport of nutrients and waste via blood plasma. Notably, the brain, approximately 73% water, depends heavily on proper hydration to maintain cognitive functions and regulatory processes.

Blood, roughly 90% water, is essential for oxygen delivery via haemoglobin, immune response through white blood cells, and clotting via platelets. Every cellular process depends on water as its medium; without sufficient intake, biological functions deteriorate rapidly, leading to dehydration and illness.

Historically, ancient Egyptian civilisation focused on the Nile River’s vital water cycle, which supported agriculture, transportation, and societal stability for thousands of years. Similarly, the Ayurvedic treatise Charaka Samhita (around 1500 BCE) systematically classified water sources by origin — rain, rivers, wells, springs — and by seasonality, documenting their medicinal and therapeutic qualities long before modern hydrology was formalised.

The River in Motion

Blood extends
the foundation further.

If water is a river, blood is that river in perpetual motion — alive, purposeful, and vital. It consists of about 55% plasma, which is roughly 90% water, and 45% cellular components — red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.

The heart propels approximately 5 litres of blood through nearly 100,000 kilometres of vessels every minute — a cycle repeated over 2.5 billion times during an average lifetime, tirelessly maintaining tissue health and continuously supplying oxygen, nutrients, and immune components.

Side by Side

Two fluids
with a shared origin.

Water

External to the body

Cycles invisibly — evaporating from oceans, lakes, and soil, condensing into clouds, precipitating as rain, flowing into rivers, and ultimately returning as freshwater in your glass. It forms part of Earth’s endless water cycle, driven by solar energy, evaporation, condensation, and runoff.

Blood

Internal to the body

Cycles invisibly too — oxygen molecules inhaled into the lungs are transported via blood to tissues, while carbon dioxide is expelled through respiration; this process repeats endlessly. The body’s internal weather system, regulating and maintaining internal stability.

“Water surrounds us constantly and invisibly. Blood performs an identical pattern within our bodies — moving with the same relentless rhythm.”

The Shared Rhythm

Named differently.
One vital movement.

Although called differently, these processes depict the same vital movement. Yoruba tradition refers to this as “ase,” while Ayurveda describes it as “prana.” Both terms symbolise a life force carried within the blood — akin to water riding the wind — spiritually revered in one tradition, scientifically measured in another, yet fundamentally representing the same energetic flow.

Water is life around us, nourishing ecosystems and the planet. Blood embodies this life within us, sustaining cellular vitality and health through continuous motion.

The Ancient Record

Healers studied one
to understand the other.

Throughout history, civilisations have meticulously documented these fluids; no enduring tradition with written records has treated water and blood as entirely separate entities. Healers and scholars studied one to understand the other — because, in practice, the body makes it impossible to separate their roles.

How water was studied

By source and season

Ancient Ayurvedic texts categorised water by origin — rain, rivers, wells, springs — and linked each to specific healing and therapeutic uses. Roman engineers built aqueducts based on practical observations that clean, flowing water prevents disease caused by stagnant, polluted sources.

How blood was studied

By quality and flow

Ibn Sina’s Canon of Medicine (1025 CE) described blood as “thin,” “thick,” or “hot,” recommending dietary and lifestyle adjustments to balance these qualities — principles that underpin modern haematology.

The lesson across time and cultures remains clear: the fluid outside the body and the fluid inside respond to similar care and respect. Clean water is crucial for healthy blood; neglecting either compromises overall health.

When the Balance Is Disrupted

Both fluids give
warning signs.

Both fluids give warning signs when their harmony is disturbed.

Water depletion

Plasma volume decreases

This thickens the blood. The heart works harder to circulate it. Even a 2% loss of body water can impair cognitive functions like concentration, sometimes before you feel thirsty.

Blood nutritional deficit

Oxygen delivery drops

Iron-deficiency anaemia affects about 1.6 billion people worldwide, causing symptoms such as fatigue, shortness of breath, and pallor — indicators of low iron reserves.

The solutions for both issues are similar: access to clean water, eating iron-rich foods, and allowing the body time to replenish its reserves.

One fluid.
Two forms. One truth.

Water and blood are vital parts of the same interconnected system. Caring for one means caring for the other. Drinking clean water nourishes the blood and supports overall vitality.

Remember: we are deeply connected to nature. Maintain the delicate balance, and health will follow.
