The Foundation

A superfood
celebrated across time.

The sweet potato is a high nutrient density “superfood”, celebrated across cultures for its vitamins A, C, fibre, and antioxidants. Historically used in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues including diarrhoea, constipation, indigestion, and bloating — the sweet potato was understood as medicine long before science gave it a profile.

In Jamaica and across the Caribbean, sweet potato is a ground provision — a staple of the ancestral diet that has sustained communities for generations. It is not an exotic health food. It is home food. The science just caught up with what grandmothers already knew.

“High nutrient density makes sweet potatoes a superfood — celebrated for vitamins A, C, fibre, and antioxidants that the body can actually use, meal after meal.”

Vitamin A is critical for good eyesight, immune defences, and organ function. Sweet potato is one of the richest plant sources of beta-carotene on earth — the compound the body converts to vitamin A on demand, without the toxicity risk of preformed vitamin A from animal sources.

Digestion

A prebiotic that
feeds your gut.

Sweet potatoes contain high levels of dietary fibre, acting as a prebiotic that promotes beneficial gut bacteria — specifically Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium. These bacteria are among the most studied and health-supportive in the human microbiome.

Sweet potatoes support digestive health by encouraging these bacteria and reducing inflammation, helping maintain a healthy gut barrier — the thin lining that separates the gut contents from the bloodstream, and whose integrity is fundamental to immune function, mood regulation, and nutrient absorption.

Historically, sweet potatoes have been used in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues including diarrhoea, constipation, indigestion, and bloating. Traditional medicine arrived at this understanding centuries before clinical trials confirmed it.

Immunity

Boosting the body’s
natural defences.

The immune-supporting profile of sweet potato is multi-layered. Vitamin A maintains the integrity of mucosal surfaces — the body’s first-line physical barriers against pathogens. Vitamin C supports the production and function of white blood cells. Together, they create a comprehensive immune defence from a single whole food.

Beyond vitamins, sweet potatoes contain an array of phytonutrients — compounds that plants produce for their own defence, but which benefit human health when consumed. These are compounds that cannot be replicated in a supplement because they work synergistically with each other and with the food matrix they come from.

Research

Anti-cancer
potential.

Research suggests bioactive compounds in sweet potatoes may slow or stop cancer cell growth, especially in red varieties rich in anthocyanins — the same compounds that give red and purple produce their colour. Studies have looked at effects on breast, stomach, and colon cancer cells.

These phytochemicals, with antioxidant effects, help reduce cell damage caused by free radicals — unstable molecules that damage DNA and contribute to cancer risk over time. Sweet potato anthocyanins have shown the ability to inhibit the proliferation of cancer cell lines in laboratory settings. This is not a cure. It is a protective food. Eaten consistently, as part of an ancestral diet, it does what nature designed it to do.

Phytonutrients

The compounds
science is still learning.

Phytonutrients are the frontier of nutritional science — compounds identified in the last few decades that traditional and ancestral diets have delivered for millennia. Sweet potato is rich in all of them.

Beta-carotene
Precursor to vitamin A. Antioxidant. Orange pigment — the deeper the colour, the higher the concentration.
Lutein
Protects the eyes from oxidative damage. Associated with reduced risk of macular degeneration and cataracts.
Zeaxanthin
Works with lutein for eye health. Also has anti-inflammatory properties throughout the body.
Anthocyanins
Predominantly in purple/red varieties. Potent antioxidants with documented anti-cancer and anti-inflammatory activity.
Flavonoids
Broad class of anti-inflammatory, antioxidant compounds that protect blood vessels and support cardiovascular health.
Tannins
Support gut health and have antimicrobial properties. Work synergistically with fibre to support the intestinal barrier.

Ground provision.
Ancient superfood.

The sweet potato does not need a marketing campaign. It has been growing from the earth, feeding communities, and protecting bodies for thousands of years. Add it to your plate. Eat it regularly. Understand what it is doing for you.
