Tropical Fruits and Documented Benefits

Every fruit a function.Every function documented.

Soursop (Annona muricata) contains acetogenins — compounds studied extensively for their biological activity. It is also rich in vitamin C, B vitamins, and fibre. Naseberry (sapodilla) provides iron, copper, folate, and niacin alongside natural sugars delivered with fibre, producing a slower glycaemic response than refined sugar. Guinep provides vitamins A and C, iron, calcium, and phosphorus in a small, intensely flavoured package. Star apple provides calcium — unusually high for a tropical fruit — alongside vitamin C and dietary fibre. The diversity of Jamaican tropical fruit represents a nutritional spectrum that no supplement programme replicates.

Nuts and Seeds

Grown here.Eaten here. Documented everywhere.

Breadnut (the seed of the breadfruit tree, also called chataigne) provides complete protein, omega-3 fatty acids, and significant iron — roasted or boiled, it is one of the most nutritionally complete plant foods available in Jamaica. Cashew apple — the fruit attached to the cashew nut — is extraordinarily rich in vitamin C (five times more than an orange by weight) and is widely available in Jamaica yet rarely discussed in nutritional terms. Pumpkin seeds, widely available across the island, provide 8mg of iron per 100g — among the highest plant sources documented — alongside zinc, magnesium, and omega-3 fatty acids. The Jamaican food environment is an extraordinary natural pharmacy. The knowledge required to use it is what this platform exists to restore.
